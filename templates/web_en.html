Learning and commitment

Designed and developed to understand the territory.

Cuyum was created for schools and universities. It connects programming, networking and live-data concepts with open and experimental seismological records, all within a system that can be fully understood and reinvented from within.

The monitor interface is designed to run 24/7 on multiple refurbished devices and embedded systems such as the ESP32. Experimental notices can also be received through the Telegram channel @redcuyum.

With minimal adjustments, Cuyum can monitor any part of the world through the Internet and reorganize its cells according to the stations available.

Frequently asked questions

Its purpose and operation

What inspired the creation of Cuyum?
Cuyum was created to help explain how seismic signals propagate across a territory. It brings together programming, networking, open data and experimental observation in an educational tool. It does not replace official agencies: it makes the behavior of a seismic network visible and accessible for study.

Can I use Cuyum on an old phone?
Yes. An old phone, tablet or laptop can remain connected to power with the monitor open in a browser. No application needs to be installed. The device only needs continuous Internet access to update the data in real time. You can also join the Telegram channel @redcuyum to receive experimental notices in real time.

Why can it work anywhere in the world?
Because Cuyum does not depend on a fixed location. The system adopts a geographic center and organizes the available stations around that point. By changing the configuration, it can adapt to another city or region, provided that open and accessible seismic stations are available.

How can I deploy it in my community?
You need a cloud server (VPS) with 2 GB of RAM, Ubuntu, Docker, optionally your own domain, and open seismic stations in the territory you want to monitor. You can configure the coordinates of your center and validate events for several days. Both the monitor and the website can be fully adapted to your community.

What information can Cuyum display?
Cuyum displays available sensors, observation zones, detected signals and estimated propagation times toward the configured center. It also preserves experimental records of multisignals confirmed by the network. The firmware and documentation are available so that you can build your own Cuyum observer with an ESP32 and receive experimental notices around the clock.

What information does Cuyum not determine?
Cuyum does not calculate magnitudes, determine epicenters or replace information from official agencies. Its records are experimental and must be compared with the publications of INPRES and other seismological institutions.

Would you like to replicate Cuyum for your area?

As an open and experimental project, the code is published under the GNU GPL v3.0 or later. This initiative does not replace INPRES or other official agencies.
